# Hypothesis Tree

Decision-grade research starts by framing the decision, not by searching. The hypothesis
tree is the frame: it forces research to be driven by what would actually change the
choice, so effort goes to the few questions that move the decision rather than to whatever
is easiest to google.

## Build it before searching

1. **State the decision.** One sentence with the real options, e.g. "Enter the mid-market
   healthcare segment in the next 12 months, or stay focused on retail." A research task
   with no decision is a topic survey — route those elsewhere.
2. **Name the candidate answers.** Usually 2–4 mutually exclusive options.
3. **Decompose each into the claims it depends on.** For "enter healthcare", the
   load-bearing claims might be: the segment is large enough, we can reach buyers, our
   product fits their compliance needs, and the economics beat staying.
4. **Turn each claim into a testable hypothesis** with a falsifiable form: "Mid-market
   healthcare TAM reachable in 12 months exceeds $X." A hypothesis you cannot disconfirm is
   not yet sharp enough.

## Prioritize by decision-impact

Tag every hypothesis with how much resolving it would move the decision (high / medium /
low) and how uncertain it currently is. Research the **high-impact, high-uncertainty**
hypotheses first; these are the questions whose answers flip the recommendation. Low-impact
or already-settled hypotheses are logged but not chased.

## Hand-off

The tree becomes the spine of the whole report: each parallel research track answers one or
more hypotheses, the evidence ledger links sources to hypotheses, and the final
recommendation is justified by which hypotheses survived. Record which hypotheses are
resolved, which remain open, and which were never load-bearing after all.
