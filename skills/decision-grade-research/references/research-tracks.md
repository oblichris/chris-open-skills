# Parallel Research Tracks

Once the hypothesis tree exists, research runs as a small set of parallel tracks rather
than one undirected search. Each track owns a slice of the question, captures its sources
as it goes, and reports back against specific hypotheses. Parallel tracks reduce anchoring:
no single line of evidence dominates just because it was found first.

## A typical track set

- **Track A — Market & demand**: size, growth, buyer needs, willingness to pay. Resolves
  the "is the opportunity real and large enough" hypotheses.
- **Track B — Execution & fit**: can we build, deliver, and support it; product/compliance
  fit; required capabilities. Resolves the "can we actually do it" hypotheses.
- **Track C — Economics & competition**: unit economics, incumbents, switching costs,
  competitive response. Resolves the "does it pay, and can we win" hypotheses.
- **Track D — Adversarial** (see `adversarial-track-d.md`): explicitly tries to falsify the
  leading answer. Always run last and always run it.

## Discipline within a track

- Capture every source the moment it informs a claim: URL or file path, title, date, and
  the specific hypothesis it speaks to. This feeds the evidence ledger directly — do not
  defer source capture to the end.
- Tag the strength of each finding as `observed`, `estimated`, or `assumed`. A track built
  mostly on `assumed` is flagging that the decision is resting on belief, not evidence.
- Prefer primary and local-captured sources over volatile secondary links, so the research
  is reproducible later.

## Reporting back

Each track returns: which hypotheses it resolved, the direction of the evidence, the
strongest supporting and strongest contradicting sources, and any new hypothesis it
surfaced. Contradictions between tracks are not averaged away — they are handed to conflict
adjudication.
